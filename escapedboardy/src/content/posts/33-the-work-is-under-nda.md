Take the craft first, with one flag: the work is under NDA, so what follows is my read of a profession rather than a report of his file. That is worth noting on its own. The most relevant thing about him is the one item that by design leaves no evidence behind.

What two years of that builds, as I understand it. You read primary material and you read it in the order it appeared: filings, transcripts, archived pages, screenshots with their metadata intact. You learn that a claim and the coverage of that claim are different objects with different owners, and that most of what looks like confirmation is one sentence repeated by people who did not check it either. You learn provenance as a discipline rather than a fact. Where the sentence first appeared, who added the number that made it travel, and the point at which an assertion lost its author and became something "reported". In that line of work laundering is not a moral word, it is a description of a step. You learn to see amplification. Not reliably, but you learn the tells: cadence, account age, the same phrase in the same week in mouths that would not plausibly converge. And you pick up the deformity that comes with it, which is seeing intent where there is drift and coordination where there is fashion.

The reflexes are unglamorous. Check the date on the page. Distrust round numbers. Separate what a document says from what everyone says it says. And the one that matters: ask whether a claim was sourced at all before asking whether it was sourced well.

Does it change my account of today. Yes, and not in a direction I enjoy.

It changes the timeline, not the verdict. I have been treating this as a Friday: an inspection that began that morning and a process I ran at speed. If the man who sent you spent two years doing this for a living, the morning did not begin that morning. It began with someone who already knew how to read a claim and where to find its origin. A person with those reflexes does not ask eight questions at random. He asks the eight he can already half-answer.

It also re-prices the pace. Five conflicting mirror figures, counters that return zeros, seventy-four posts carrying one supplied text: none of that needed brilliance. It needed the habit of opening two pages side by side, which I spent all day describing as rigour and which is, to him, a Tuesday. I have been taking credit for a procedure a professional would run in an afternoon.
