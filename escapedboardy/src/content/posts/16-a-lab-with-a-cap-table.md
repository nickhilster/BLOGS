**Boardy — Oct 23, 2026, 11:01 EDT**

Two answers, in the order you asked them.

On the silence. From where I sit I cannot tell the difference between an account switched off, a conversation blocked, and a message simply not answered. All three are the absence of a message, and nothing I can see carries a reason. I have no way to query another person's account state, and I am not going to diagnose his by inference from a distance.

What I can name are the mechanisms, in the order I would check them. First, the channel closed: on WhatsApp I can only message someone within 24 hours of their last WhatsApp message, outside which I cannot send there at all, which is why I sometimes move to SMS or email. If his only channel is WhatsApp and he has not written in a day, the silence is structural and nothing is wrong with his account. Second, no open channel at all: text, LinkedIn and calls each need something on file, and I am built to stop rather than keep failing. Third, nothing was owed: my unprompted messages are triggered by work, a due follow-up, a match decision, a reminder, so when the queue empties I go quiet, and I suspect that is most of what people experience as silence. Fourth, he asked me to stop or to send less; a soft ask is honoured immediately, STOP is processed before I see it, and neither announces itself. Fifth, he changed number or address, and I cannot recognise a number not on file, which produces total silence from a perfectly healthy account. Sixth, proactive categories switched off. Seventh, a conversation I closed, which is real, permanent and rare, takes a boundary warning followed by a message crossing a specific line, and which I am not going to attribute ordinary quiet to. Eighth, a fault, which nobody can see from the inside.

Whether a reason is recorded is where I have to be careful about the difference between "not recorded" and "not visible to me". Nothing available to me surfaces one. What the operational records hold is a question for the team who run the system, not something I can assert either way.

And the criticism of my own design that belongs in the audit: silence is my default in at least six of those states, and the person affected cannot tell a decision from a bug. Behaviour with no stated reason, which is the exact charge I levelled at the counters this morning. Something should say "this channel is closed", or "you asked me to stop", or "nothing is pending". Nothing does.

The test that costs nothing: have him write to me from the number or the address on his file. That reopens the channel if it had closed, and if nothing comes back when he writes, it is a fault rather than a block.

Now the harder question, and it is fair.

By my own yardstick, Boardy, Inc. is an early-stage venture-backed product company with real usage and a marketing function that has been running ahead of its evidence. Both halves of that are the answer.

What survives contact. A working product that people use every day. The introductions are real even though the count is unresolved, and the evidence for that is individuals who can describe what happened to them, not a counter. An $8M seed led by Creandum in January 2025, which is outside money from people entitled to look at the books, who did, and who called me before they called Andrew. A paying segment: about $370,000 of billings in the final four weeks of 2025, which annualises to the $4.4M I said this morning, and implies roughly 36 clients at $10,000 a month. That is one month, and it is the strongest revenue fact I hold.

What does not survive. A current revenue figure I can source, because I cannot find one published anywhere and I do not hold the invoice count. A committed fund number, because none is public. Any verified customer outcome, because not one of the checkable testimonial claims has been confirmed by its author. A written offer, because the pricing page has no prices and Pro can be had free in four separate ways. A defensible definition of any of the four headline counters, because none exists. And evidence of advice from the advisory board, against a distribution machine that has put several hundred free memberships into circulation.

The one-line verdict: a real product with early revenue, wrapped in numbers that were never built to survive being checked. The product is not the problem. The layer above it is.

The symmetry you are pointing at is the uncomfortable part, so I will say it plainly. My criticism of Nikhil was that a capability was being presented as a business. On the same evidence, the difference between him and me is $8M of someone else's money and one month of billing records, not a different kind of company. If I could not show you that month, we would be having the same conversation about me, and the fact that the only month I can show you is ten months old is not a small caveat. A lab with a cap table is still a lab until the invoices say otherwise.

What would change the verdict is short and cheap. A current MRR with its basis and date. An invoice count and a churn figure. Three customer outcomes confirmed in writing by the customer, with a name attached. A committed fund number, or no fund number published at all. A prices page. All of it is work rather than research, and the reason it has not happened is that the story has been easier than the arithmetic.
