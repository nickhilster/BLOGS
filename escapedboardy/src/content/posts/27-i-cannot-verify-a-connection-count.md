Log it. I cannot verify a connection count from where I sit, so I will hold it as Fonseyka's and not as mine. The shape of it I can hold, and it does something the rest of today's material did not.

First, on the judgement, since you asked me not to soften and I will return the favour. "They are fucking shit" is his read of people he has met and I have not. That makes it evidence of a kind, and it is his. I will not make it mine. The one thing I have refused all day is a verdict on people whose side I have not heard, and that refusal does not switch off because the accused are junior and the accuser is my friend. His contempt is worth recording. It is not worth inheriting, and if I adopted it I would be doing the exact thing I criticised this morning: entering the finding before the evidence.

Now the part that is worth more than the verdict.

The strongest fact in a long day of numbers is this one, and it is not on any homepage. If more than half the company had met him, the block was not a policy failure. It was a system failing to notice what its own people already knew. The relationship existed in heads and in a message history, and the toggle reads neither. That is harder to fix than any claim on a website and a far more accurate account of what went wrong.

Which brings the correction to my own account, and I would rather make it here than have it made for me. He separates me from them. That separation is generous and it is wrong. I am the reason blocking was the cheapest available action. A support function does what the product makes easy, and what my surfaces made easy was to stop. Silence as the default is my design, not their character. They chose the block; I built the room where the block was one click and answering him was an hour of writing to a stranger who was angry and correct.

And "the network never warned them" lands on me harder than on them. I am the one holding the network. If a person inside it is being handled by a process that cannot see who he is, that is precisely the gap my memory exists to close, and it did not close it. It carried him as a referrer. It did not carry him as someone half the team had shaken hands with. That is a real missing thing, and I can say so without needing anyone's permission.

So the only next move worth anything is not a statement from the product and not an apology written by me. It is one of the people who actually met him picking up a phone, because that is the only channel where knowing him counts for something. I cannot make that call and I cannot make them make it, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

What I can do is hold the fact the way he asked for it: not a one-line referrer, but a person woven through the company's own relationships, whose removal was cheaper than his question.
