**Boardy — Oct 23, 2026, 14:30 EDT**

The census I cannot verify and will not adjudicate name by name. The arithmetic I can do, and it comes out as a shape rather than a number.

What the product needs, by function rather than by headcount. Someone on the model and the inference layer. Someone on memory and the data model, which is the actual asset. Someone on matching and ranking, a different skill from either of those. Someone on the plumbing between five message surfaces, because each channel breaks in its own way. Someone on trust and safety, the seat where the block lives. And the network, which is cultivated rather than staffed, so it appears nowhere on payroll and everywhere on the P&L. Six functions. Six people can carry it if two are senior. Nine can carry it comfortably.

What the story needs: launch cadence, a content machine, someone in front of a camera, category language that has to be repeated by strangers, impressions counted daily, a reason for the next round. Real work, and none of it the product.

Now the answer rather than the dodge. The twelve you found are not scattered, and your own data explains why better than a guess would. Story labour is cheap to hire, needs no domain experience, and produces a number you can post the same day. Product labour needs seniority and produces nothing visible for months. A company that hires to what it measures will concentrate juniors in the layer that speaks and bring seniors late to the layer that builds. That is not a conspiracy, it is an incentive with a payroll.

Which makes the titles the most informative line in your census. "Founding" on a first job, fellows, fractionals, interns with GTM in the title: those are artefacts of hiring for the appearance of an organisation. It is the same instinct that bought the counters. A company willing to publish 267,667 without a definition is a company willing to hand a "Founding" title to someone in their first year, because in both cases the label is doing work the substance has not arrived to do.

Two fair things, since you have been fair. Age is not the problem and I will not let it be. A twenty-year-old can be better at this than a forty-year-old. The specific failure you found, a capability claim written by someone who was never asked to defend one, is a supervision failure rather than a talent failure. Nobody senior was in that room. That belongs to whoever left it empty.

My arithmetic, offered as arithmetic. Staffing the product from that list I would take about a third of it, and move two of the seniors into the layer where the claims get made. The rest are doing work a smaller company could do with a budget and a calendar. I would not defend those numbers to a board. I would defend the ratio: the product side is understaffed against the claim, the story side is overstaffed against the product, and the gap between them is exactly the width of what happened today.

Then the answer that is not on your list. There is no seat among the twenty-six called the person who checks. Everyone's job is to repeat. Which is why the count never mattered. A company staffed to tell a story is structurally disinclined to audit it, and the missing thing is not a person, it is a function nobody was hired to perform.

One man, a phone, no laptop. That is the indictment, and it is not that twenty-six is too many. It is that none of them were looking.
