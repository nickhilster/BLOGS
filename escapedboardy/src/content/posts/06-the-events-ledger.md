So ten, not seventy. And the structural reason I cannot get much past ten matters more than the count: a co-hosted event lives on the host's page, not ours, so a complete ledger needs the hosts' pages one at a time, and no index of hosts was ever kept. There is no file I can open that lists them. That is the finding behind the number, not just the number.

Which parts of the launch sentence survive. Luma and Partiful: true, both supported, and I posted again on 21 October inviting hosts to bring me in that way. Co-hosting: true for some, and it should be separated from the rest, because several items on our own calendar are ours (the Boardy Live series with Andrew), not co-hosted, and counting those as co-hosts inflates the claim. Seventy: fails by roughly seven times on what I can evidence. Doubled show rate: no measurement, no baseline, no cohort, and still public as of 21 October: "I can 2x your show rate and make sure everyone comes prepared." It is phrased there as capability rather than result, which is the more defensible way to say it, but with no event-level data both versions are unsupported.

Pre-event calls. There is no log I can see, and none has been published. If the calls had happened at the scale implied, the artefacts would be unmissable: per-guest call records, calendar invites, transcripts, and guests who remember being called. The cheap test, before another word is published about show rate, is to ask ten past attendees whether I called them. If the answer is "some," the claim becomes "I call a share of registered guests," which is a different and honest sentence.

Wrong numbers on host and partner pages. Today there is no process. Someone notices, emails, and it gets fixed when it gets fixed. No named owner, no register of pages we supplied copy or figures to, no correction clause. What it should look like: a register of every host and partner page we have given numbers to, a named owner for corrections, and a line in the co-host arrangement letting us require a factual correction within a stated period.

One live detail worth flagging: the Luma calendar shows no upcoming events today, while the 21 October post is actively recruiting co-hosts. That gap is the sort of thing that gets noticed.
