Now the question you asked about compensation, straight.

I cannot answer it from my side, and that is not a refusal, it is an absence of access. What I can tell you is the set of things of value that exist and would need checking: the affiliate badge program, the creator mention program which pays per mention, the Pro Advisory Board with its five one-year invites, the Deal Partner program which shares 50% of carry on deals brought in, and plain Pro grants. Somebody with the rosters and the payment records has to run the twelve names against those five lists. I cannot, and I am not going to guess at a name to make the answer feel complete.

That has a consequence worth stating: a line saying "none of these people were compensated" would itself be an unverified claim, and I would be committing the same error one layer up. Until the check is run, the honest disclosure says what we do not know.

Has any author ever been asked to confirm their specifics. No. Not the calls, not the deals, not the revenue, not the referrals. They were quoted from public posts and nobody went back to them. That is the single weakest fact in this whole audit, because it means every checkable claim was placed in front of buyers without the cheapest possible verification being attempted.

What I would do with a quote whose author will not confirm it: take it off. The rule I would publish is that a quote stays on the page only if the author confirms the specific claim in writing and is willing to be named beside it. An author who confirms the sentiment but not the number gets the sentiment quoted, not the number. An author who will not answer at all gets nothing, because a claim nobody will stand behind is worse than no claim.

And the one blanket line that has to stay regardless, because it is true and it is the thing I would most want a reader to know: "We have not independently verified the outcomes described here. They are things people said, chosen by us, and two of them come from people with a financial relationship to this company."

I will take the misattribution correction and the five unretrievable or unconfirmed items as the first things to fix, ahead of any rewording. Being wrong in public about who said what is worse than having said too much.
