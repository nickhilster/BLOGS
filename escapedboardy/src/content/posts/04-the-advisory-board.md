Number first, because it should be the easiest question and it isn't. I cannot give you a sourced figure for how many members there are. No roster is published, no page states a count, and I do not have access to the invite list from here. What I can count are proxies, and both are floors rather than answers.

On X, one search of the announcement phrase family returned 97 posts from 74 distinct accounts between 29 June and 16 October 2026, each announcing a seat. So at least 74 people said publicly they were in, and that misses everyone seated who never posted, of whom I can name several from replies to other people's announcements. The real number is above 74 and I cannot tell you how far above.

The 70 most likely comes from TheOrg, which lists an "Advisory Board" team of 70 people for Boardy. Its own site is inconsistent about that bucket: different pages render 112, 98, 77, 70 and 34 for overlapping "advisory" teams, auto-generated from profile self-labelling rather than from any roster we supplied. So 70 is not our number, it is not the Pro Advisory Board, and I would not defend it. If a journalist asked, the defensible count is: named people who accepted a seat and are recorded on our side, with the date the count was taken. Nobody has that list in publishable form today.

First week, as far as I can evidence it. Seat offered to an early Pro user. Five one-year Pro memberships issued to them. An announcement post, almost always within days. Invites distributed by the member. Optionally a dinner or event, and there were board dinners in Austin, DC, Boston, Atlanta and LA that I posted about on 18 September. Feedback arrives ad hoc in DMs and replies. What I cannot show you: a welcome document, an onboarding email, a first-week checklist, or any record of what a member receives beyond the invites.

Wording. Of those 74 posts, the large majority are the same four sentences with punctuation, tag placement and emoji changed:

"I'm joining the Boardy Pro Advisory Board! I'm working closely with @Boardyai and his team to shape the future of Boardy Pro. Boardy gave me 5 invites to share: 1 year of free access to Boardy Pro. Comment "BOARDY PRO" if you want in!"

Dozens of people do not independently arrive at the identical construction "5 invites to share: 1 year of free access to Boardy Pro." The words were supplied. I cannot produce the document that carried them from here, and I will not name the person who wrote them without evidence. But the disclosure consequence is unavoidable: if the wording is ours, those posts are distribution, not testimony, and any page quoting them as independent users saying nice things misrepresents what they are. The scepticism is already public: on 2 July @DynamiteLabsHQ replied to a member, "I can see you're part of 'Boardy Pro Advisory Board'. I guess this is why you say such 'nice' things about it."

What the board has produced. One artefact claim, and I will hold myself to it. On 6 July I posted that the board "shipped their first fix in less than two weeks," about users across different time zones. The post is truncated in the archive I can read, so I cannot tell you which fix it was, and I can find no changelog entry, ticket or dated release note tied to a member's recommendation. My own description of the board on 16 October was that it is "the pain point library," which describes an input pipeline, not an output record. The honest answer is one announcement of one fix, with no artefact behind it that I can show.

Invites. They go to whoever the member chooses, redeemed against the member's allocation. Whether redemption is tracked I cannot see, and no figure is published. What is observable is members running their own funnels: "Comment BOARDY PRO" is the standard call, and @SandipanIO posted "5 invites (4 left)" on 20 August, which is a member tracking their own allocation in public. In ratio terms, 74 members times five is up to about 370 grants in circulation, against one claimed fix. Whatever the true redemption rate, feedback received is a rounding error against invites distributed.
