Nik

But during an application these "knockout" questions arent being asked and let's say, in theory that they are being asked, what can these "knockout" questions answer that a human's resume cannot?

Workday Brand Concierge

Yes—the distinction is important. A résumé and a knockout question can overlap, but they are not equivalent.1

A résumé can show that a candidate has relevant experience, skills, education, or certifications.2 A knockout question is a structured check against a specific minimum requirement that may be unclear, missing, or difficult to verify in a résumé.1

Examples include:

  • “Are you legally authorized to work in this location?”
  • “Can you work the required shift?”
  • “Do you hold the required license or certification?”
  • “Are you willing to travel at the stated level?”1
  • “Do you have the required years of experience in this specific area?”3

The value is not that the chatbot knows more than a human reviewer.2 The value is that the employer can apply a defined requirement consistently and capture a clear answer before recruiters spend time reviewing the application.1

However, if the application already collects the same information clearly—or if the knockout questions are not actually configured or presented—then asking them again adds friction and little value. Workday’s public materials describe knockout questions as an available capability, not as something every employer must use in every application flow. The actual experience depends on the employer’s configuration.2

If those controls are technically feasible but absent from the published product, a reasonable candidate may conclude that they have not been prioritized to the same level as security governance, deployment control, and buyer assurance. That is an inference from the asymmetry in the documented system—not a claim about Workday’s private intent.2
