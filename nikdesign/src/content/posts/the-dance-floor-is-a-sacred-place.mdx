I was sitting at the ISKCON temple recently, listening to someone give a pravachan, when I had a strange thought.

This reminds me of DJing.

Not because there was music playing. Not because there was some obvious connection between a temple and a nightclub. It was something about the way the person speaking was holding the room.

He wasn't simply delivering information. He was telling stories from his own life, drawing from things he had experienced and understood personally. But he was also watching the people in front of him. The same story belonged to him, yet somehow it was being offered to everyone in the room in a way that allowed each person to find their own meaning in it.

Personalized to himself.

Personalized to the audience.

That's DJing.

At least, that's what DJing has always meant to me.

## If DJing is technical, the machine wins

I've been thinking about what happens to DJs as machines get better.

If DJing is fundamentally a technical profession, then I don't think we have much of an argument.

A machine can beatmatch better than I can. It can analyze keys, tempo, phrasing and thousands of tracks simultaneously. Give it computer vision and it can watch a dance floor. Give it microphones and sensors and it can listen to the room. Give it enough history and it can learn that when I see this kind of crowd responding this way at this particular moment, I tend to make this kind of decision.

Even my intuition isn't magic.

What I call instinct is, at least partly, an enormous collection of patterns accumulated over years.

So eventually a machine should be able to learn those patterns too.

And if that's all DJing is, then fine.

Build the machine.

It'll probably mix better than me.

But I don't think that's all DJing is.

## The person behind the decks

A DJ brings something into the room before the first track is ever played.

Themselves.

Their history. Their taste. Their heartbreak. Their joy. The records they discovered at three in the morning fifteen years ago and somehow never forgot. The song somebody once played for them. The music they hated until one strange night when suddenly they understood it.

That's why that pravachan stayed with me.

The person speaking wasn't trying to remove himself from the message in order to make it universally applicable. He was doing almost the opposite. He was making the message more personal so that other people could find themselves inside it.

That's an interesting contradiction.

The more honestly you bring yourself into something, the more room you sometimes create for somebody else to enter it.

That's how I think about music.

*Mi casa es su casa.*

My home is your home.

Maybe there's a DJ version of that.

My taste is your taste.

Your taste is my taste.

Not because we necessarily like the same records. We probably don't.

It means: come into my world for a while, and I'll come into yours.

Meet me somewhere in the middle.

Let's see where we end up.

## Everyone is having a different night

A dance floor is a very strange place when you really think about it.

Hundreds or thousands of people can occupy exactly the same physical space, hearing exactly the same music, and still be experiencing completely different realities.

Someone just fell in love.

Someone just got dumped.

Someone is celebrating.

Someone is lonely.

Someone came with ten friends.

Someone came alone.

Someone is trying desperately to stop thinking.

Someone has finally started thinking about something they've been avoiding for years.

Someone is fighting a battle with the world.

Someone is fighting a battle inside their own head.

And for a few hours, somehow, all of these people agree to stand together in the same room and move.

I think that's sacred.

Not sacred because a nightclub is a temple.

Sacred because there aren't many places where human beings allow themselves that degree of vulnerability in front of strangers.

You move your body.

You close your eyes.

You look ridiculous.

You stop caring.

You criticize yourself.

You lose yourself.

You find yourself.

You love yourself.

You learn something about yourself.

And then the next record comes in.

## The mix isn't the point

This is why I've never been able to think about DJing purely as mixing music.

The mix matters.

Of course it fucking matters.

But technique exists in service of something else.

A doctor makes technical decisions constantly. Those decisions aren't made for the glory of technique. The doctor isn't sitting there thinking about how beautifully medical their decision was.

The decision is for the patient.

DJing obviously isn't medicine, and I'm not pretending DJs are doctors.

I'm talking about the relationship between craft and purpose.

Technique serves the person.

For a DJ, that means every transition, every tempo change, every unexpected record, every moment you deliberately hold something back and every moment you finally let it go exists because you're trying to create an experience for the people standing in front of you.

Sometimes the right technical decision might even be the technically imperfect one.

Because you're not mixing two waveforms.

You're moving a room.

## The cook

Maybe cooking is the better analogy.

You can own every ingredient in the world.

The finest meat. The best vegetables. Every masala and spice imaginable. The expensive cookware. The perfect kitchen.

None of that means you can cook.

Because having ingredients isn't the skill.

Knowing what to do with them is.

How much.

In what order.

At what temperature.

What needs more time.

What needs less.

What should never have been added in the first place.

And eventually, after doing it long enough, you stop measuring every teaspoon.

You know.

DJing taught me that.

And looking back, I think it taught me something much larger than music.

I have accumulated a strange collection of skills over my life. Design. Technology. Storytelling. Animation. Systems thinking. Product development. Research. Events. Music. LLMs. Building things. Breaking things. Starting over.

For a long time I thought the value was in accumulating the ingredients.

Now I think DJing may have taught me the more important skill:

how to cook.

Creating something with what you have is the fucking key.

## Maybe that's why it bothers me

DJing isn't on my résumé.

That's strange when I think about it.

Because if someone asked me which skill has influenced everything else I do, DJing would probably be near the top.

Maybe at the top.

It taught me to read a room without asking the room what it wanted.

It taught me that giving people exactly what they expect isn't the same thing as giving them a good experience.

It taught me pacing.

It taught me restraint.

It taught me that sometimes you need to let something breathe.

It taught me that anticipation can be more powerful than delivery.

It taught me that people can experience the same thing completely differently.

It taught me that technical perfection and emotional impact are not the same thing.

It taught me that taste isn't about imposing your preferences on somebody else. It's about offering them something you believe in strongly enough that they're willing to follow you somewhere unfamiliar.

And eventually I stopped only DJing.

I started organizing events.

Then DJing became one ingredient inside a larger experience: the room, the people, the sequence, the atmosphere, the expectation, the story.

Which, looking back now, sounds suspiciously like everything I've been doing ever since.

## Spread Love, Spread Music

I already have a philosophy for DJing.

I've had it for years.

**Spread Love, Spread Music.**

I used to think of that mostly as a statement about sharing music.

I don't anymore.

The music is the medium.

The important part is what happens between people because of it.

My job behind the decks isn't to demonstrate how much music I know. It isn't to prove that my taste is better than yours. It isn't to perform technical competence for other DJs standing at the side of the booth with their arms crossed.

It's to create a space.

A space where you can be yourself.

Find yourself.

Criticize yourself.

Love yourself.

Know yourself.

Grow yourself.

And maybe, for one night, encounter some part of yourself that wasn't there when you walked in.

Everyone on that floor will take a different journey.

That's the beautiful part.

I don't need to decide where yours ends.

I just need to care deeply about where we go together.

**Spread Love. Spread Music.**
