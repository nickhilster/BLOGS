## One word took a darker route

**BLITZ.**

<div class="sport-atlas"><div class="sport-tile football" data-symbol="⚡"><small>German → war → football → everyday speech</small><div><h3>Blitz</h3><p>German for lightning. Associated with Blitzkrieg and the Blitz in World War II; later used for a sudden defensive rush in American football, and for an intense campaign in everyday speech.</p></div></div></div>

That one is a reminder: not every expression began on a playing field. Some arrived there carrying history.

## The words don't respect borders

A phrase can begin as a literal move, become a metaphor, cross an ocean, and end up in a meeting. Sport is a remarkable distributor of language—not necessarily the inventor of every phrase it popularizes.

And some expressions we associate with a sport have older or disputed origins. **Hat trick**, for example, is strongly associated with hockey today, but its recorded sporting origin is cricket. **On thin ice** is not a hockey invention. **Move the goalposts** is shared across goal-based sports rather than belonging cleanly to one.

That's what makes the journey interesting.

*We play games with rules. Then we take their language into a world that has none.*
